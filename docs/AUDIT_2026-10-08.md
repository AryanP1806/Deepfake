# DFIR — Adversarial Product Audit (2026-10-08)

Scope: the whole product, judged as an investigation and response workflow for a victim, journalist, investigator,
police officer, lawyer and forensic analyst. The audit is grounded in the code as it is today (`backend/dfir/**`,
`frontend/src/**`), not only the README. Where I could not verify something in code, I say so.

Notation:
- **[code]** = verified in the source.
- **[measured]** = numbers we measured in this project.
- **[research]** = external source, listed at the end.

---

## 0. Executive verdict

DFIR is a **serious evidence workbench wrapped around a detector that it presents too confidently**. Several
things are genuinely good and rare:
- the claim model with explicit status and limitations;
- the write-once vault plus custody ledger;
- screenshot-aware detection;
- the "earliest discovered ≠ original" discipline;
- the safety gate for minors;
- user-submits-only reporting.

But four things would hurt a real person today:

1. **The top of every case is a percentage verdict ("Real · 99%") that is not a probability, can say "Real"**
   about content no detector can assess (miscaptioned photos, blended faces Hive misses), and contradicts the
   README's own principle that absence of evidence is never "authentic".
2. **Forgeable metadata is treated as proof.** Stable-Diffusion parameters or a public reference watermark
   inserted into a real photo make the system say **"Fake — proof in the file — high confidence"**. That proof
   then propagates to the whole "family" through genealogy. This is a ready-made tool for the *liar's dividend*:
   discrediting real evidence.
3. **The outward-upload default is unsafe.** Since yesterday's change (my change), media goes to Hive
   automatically, and **the safety gate does not stop it**. Intimate images, and potentially child sexual abuse
   material, would be sent to a third party without a question.
4. **The action layer is built around detection, but most victims' next step does not depend on detection.**
   NCII removal, impersonation reports and fraud freezes do not require proving that something is fake. The
   product makes the victim wait for a forensic verdict before telling them what to do in the next 10 minutes.

**Not shippable to a real victim as-is. Very demonstrable at a hackathon** if you lead with the response workflow
and the honest evidence model, not with the detector percentage.

## 1. User journeys

### Case A — Ordinary victim (manipulated image or video of themselves)

| Step | What happens today | Does the user know what happened, what it means, and what to do? | Confusion / harm points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opens app | Case list; "New case" asks for a title, incident type and safety flags (possible minor / intimate) [code] | Partly | "Incident type" uses investigator categories. Nobody asks *"Is anyone in danger? Has money moved? Is it intimate?"* first |
| Uploads file | Hashed into the vault; the analysis panel shows "step 10 of 15: TruFor" [code] | Knows something is running; not what TruFor is | Step names (SPAI, SBI, TruFor, C2PA, "Decomposition") mean nothing to the victim. A wait of 8–60 s is spent on detection before any guidance |
| **Media is sent to Hive automatically** | Logged in custody only [code] | **No**: the user is never told their intimate image just left the laptop | 🔴 Consent and safety failure (see §8, §11) |
| Verdict card | "Likely fake · 85%", confidence, reasons, "Second opinion disagrees…" | Understands "fake". Does **not** understand 85% of *what*, or why two engines disagree | If Hive misses the fake: **"Real · 99% likely real · confidence high"**. This is what Hive returns for 5/5 of the demo blended-face fakes [measured]. A victim told "your fake is real" by a forensic tool is badly harmed |
| Sidebar: Overview, Decomposition, Genealogy, Propagation, Graph, Timeline, Leads, Gaps, Evidence, Next action, Complaint, Monitoring | 12 jargon steps | No | The steps follow the investigator's mental model, not the victim's. "Next action" is the **10th** item |
| Propagation | Paste URLs | Only if they already know the URLs | Instagram and Facebook often show a login wall to logged-out capture; failure is technical ("capture failed") rather than "do this instead" |
| Complaint | NCRP-mapped draft blocks | Mostly | The user must re-type everything into cybercrime.gov.in. Nothing says which portal route to pick for their case (Women/Child anonymous vs Other cyber crime, which needs an OTP login), or what file sizes the portal accepts |
| Evidence | ZIP with `verify_package.py`, a draft BSA s.63 certificate | No | A victim (or a police station) won't run Python. Nothing says who signs the certificate (Part A vs Part B) |
| Monitoring | Re-captures known URLs | Partly | Does not find **new** re-uploads without search API keys; the UI doesn't make this limit prominent |

**Biggest gap:** the first screen should be *"What's happening to you?"* followed by a 10-minute checklist (preserve,
report, freeze money, don't forward, StopNCII, and so on). Detection should be one card on that page, not the gate in
front of it.

### Case B — Journalist (viral image claimed to show a real event)

| Question | Does DFIR answer it? |
|---|---|
| Is it authentic? | Only the **pixel question** ("was it generated or edited?"). The most common viral falsehood is a **real photo with a false caption** (wrong place, wrong date, old event). DFIR would say **"Real"**, which is technically "no manipulation found" but reads as *"the claim is true"*. 🔴 |
| What evidence supports it? | The detector list and claims are good for an internal note. Hive's scores are a closed vendor opinion that cannot be cited as reproducible evidence |
| Where did it originate? | Only among the URLs the journalist already pasted. There's no reverse-image or first-appearance search without paid keys. "Earliest discovered ≠ original" is handled correctly [code] |
| How did it spread? | A list of locations with declared publish times. Reposts and quote-chains are not visible; displayed counts are unreliable |
| What can I safely publish? | **Not answered.** There's no publication-language guidance ("We could not independently verify…", "Experts' tools flagged…"), and no warning against re-amplifying the fake (publish a cropped, watermarked or blurred version) |

DFIR is ~40% of a verification desk's workflow. The missing 60% is context verification: reverse search,
first-appearance, geolocation and chronolocation, contacting the source, SIFT-style lateral reading.

### Case C — Cybercrime investigator

| Stage | Defensible? |
|---|---|
| Preservation | **Good base:** SHA-256 + MD5 at acquisition, write-once, per-file acquisition JSON, hash-chained ledger [code]. **Missing:** authenticated analyst identity (actor defaults to "investigator") [code]; clock verification; external anchoring of the ledger (the RFC 3161 consent exists but **is not implemented**) [code]; how the victim obtained the file (WhatsApp forward? screenshot?) |
| Forensic analysis | Interesting, but **not reproducible**: Hive is closed, versionless from our side and can change silently. Its raw response and `task_id` are **not stored**; only class scores are kept [code]. The verdict is **computed at view time** from the current calibration and config [code]. Re-open the case after re-calibrating, or with a Hive key in a different state, and the verdict can change |
| Provenance | C2PA is good. SD parameters and the watermark are treated as **proof** although they are forgeable 🔴 |
| Genealogy | Near-duplicate grouping is sound; parent→child direction is over-asserted (§4) |
| Source discovery | Public web only, with no platform-side data (correct and stated). It doesn't produce the list of *what to request from which platform* (subscriber info, upload IP logs, original upload file, account creation data) for a lawful notice. That is exactly the officer's next step |
| Relationships | Leads, labelled as leads; collection bias is not explained (§6) |
| Timeline | Good: time source, precision, unknown-creation band, conflicts [code]. Bad: user notes and monitoring alerts are labelled **Verified** [code] |
| Package | Useful structure; missing model locks, calibration, config, verdict snapshot, external timestamp and encryption (§8) |
| Next lead | The Gaps list is good in concept ("only the platform can provide X via legal request") |

**Verdict:** a defensible *collection* workflow and an indefensible *assessment* record.

### Case D — Organisation (fake CEO video or voice)

| Need | Support |
|---|---|
| Verify | **Video:** Hive only (local detectors are image-only) [code]. **Audio:** **no detector at all.** Hive isn't called for audio [code], the heuristics are bandwidth and pauses, and the verdict for audio-only files is "Not enough detectors ran". The product has a `voice_clone_scam` incident type it cannot actually assess 🔴 |
| Assess impact | Displayed counts only; no audience reach, no "who received it" (internal email, WhatsApp groups) |
| Preserve | Good |
| Find copies | Known URLs plus optional search adapters (keys required) |
| Identify accounts | Observed-posting only (correct) |
| Report and takedown | Platform report texts exist; there's **no takedown tracker with legal clocks**, and no trademark or brand-impersonation route |
| Monitor recurrence | Known URLs only |
| Document incident | The case file exists, but not in an incident-response structure (detection, containment, eradication, recovery, lessons) |
| Missing org steps | Finance freeze, call 1930, bank fraud desk, internal "verify by call-back" alert, holding statement, CERT-In reportability check, legal counsel |

## 2. DETECT audit

### What exists [code]

- **Images:** SPAI (AI-generated), TruFor (local edits), SBI (face blending), Hive V3 (AI-generated + deepfake
  faces, commercial, now primary), UFD (disabled), plus a screenshot content-region crop.
- **Video:** Hive, plus heuristic temporal signals.
- **Audio:** heuristics only.

### Are they complementary?

Partly. They answer different questions, which is good:
- SPAI → whole-image synthesis;
- TruFor → splicing and inpainting in camera photos;
- SBI → blended faces;
- Hive → both, as a black box.

But:
- **No lip-sync / reenactment / audio-visual sync detector.** That is the dominant form of political and CEO video
  deepfakes.
- **No audio-clone detector.**
- **No "real photo, false context" capability.** That isn't a detector problem; it needs search and context tools.
- Hive overlaps everything and is closed, so "agreement" between Hive and SPAI is not independent evidence if both
  learned the same generator artefacts.

### Calibration and score interpretation

| Issue | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Prior** | The local stack is fitted with balanced classes on OpenFake, so its outputs are posteriors **under a 50% prior** [code]. A victim submitting "the fake of me" has a prior near 100% that it is manipulated. A journalist screening viral images may have a 5–20% prior. The same "85%" means different things, and the UI states no prior |
| **Hive scores** | Not probabilities for our population. Hive suggests a 0.9 cut-off [research]. Hive is bimodal (≈0.00 or ≈0.99 on almost everything we sent). A 0.00 means *"the classifier did not fire"*, not *"99% real"* |
| **SBI** | Raw output fires on 48% of real faces [measured]. The percentile mapping `0.95·(pct−0.9)/0.1` is an **ad-hoc transform, not a calibrated probability** |
| **Fusion** | `p_fake = 1−(1−p_ai)(1−p_edit)` assumes the two are independent probabilities. They are neither independent nor both probabilities |
| **Held-out claims** | "Hive right 99%" is **n = 87 clean OpenFake images** (95% CI ≈ 94–99.8%), the kind of generator output Hive trains on. In the wild, commercial detectors stay under 90% accuracy and open-source models are no better than chance (AUC ≤ 0.58) on Deepfake-Eval-2024 [research]. Showing "right 99% of the time" on every Hive verdict **overgeneralises** |
| **Generator shift** | Measured: local recall on Flux.2 was 7%, Wan-video 0% [measured]. This is a structural weakness, and new generators appear monthly |
| **Compression and resizing** | SPAI is resized to ≤2048 px, TruFor to ≤1280 px [code]. Platform recompression (WhatsApp ≈ 1600 px at JPEG quality ~70) weakens all local signals. Screenshots are partly handled by the crop |
| **Screenshot crop** | A heuristic box detector [code]. A wrong crop (e.g. picking the profile banner) analyses the wrong pixels, and the card doesn't show the crop prominently enough to catch that |
| **Faces** | YuNet misses small, profile or occluded faces → SBI says nothing. Absence is reported as "the faces look ordinary" when no face was found |
| **Video** | No frame-level local detector; no temporal consistency model; no AV-sync |
| **Adversarial** | Re-photographing a screen, adding noise, upscaling, JPEG laundering, or a crop-then-pad all degrade every detector. Nothing measures robustness |

### Does the system turn probabilistic outputs into a misleading binary truth?

**Yes**, in three places:
1. The headline label ("Real", "Fake").
2. The big percentage ("99% likely real").
3. The reliability line ("right 99% of the time"), which reads like a property of *this* verdict.

### What should change

- Replace the label and percentage with an **evidence-strength statement on the manipulation question only**
  (§10). Never "Real"; use "No signs of manipulation found — this does not confirm the image is authentic or that
  the caption is true."
- Treat each detector as a **likelihood ratio estimated on a named reference set**, e.g. "On 37 fakes and 50 real
  photos like this, Hive scores ≥0.9 occurred in 36/37 fakes and 0/50 reals". Report in **natural frequencies**,
  with the reference set named.
- **Applicability gating:** before showing a detector's result, check that it applies:
  - SBI requires a detected face of ≥N px;
  - TruFor is meaningless on fully synthetic images;
  - SPAI and TruFor are unreliable below ~256 px or on screenshots of video.
  
  Non-applicable detectors show "not applicable", never a reassuring score.
- **Disagreement is a finding, not noise.** "Hive: no AI image; face-swap detector: strongly blended face" → state
  plainly "Detectors disagree — the commercial detector did not flag it, a specialised face-swap detector did.
  Treat as *suspected face swap*; get an expert review." Do not output "Real · high confidence".
- **Audio and video:** call Hive for audio (the response already contains `ai_generated_audio` classes)
  [measured]. Add an AV-sync check for talking-head video. Until then, say *"We cannot assess audio deepfakes"*.

## 3. FORENSICS audit — technique by technique

| Technique | Class | Why | Show to normal user? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C2PA, valid signature, trusted signer, AI assertion** | **Strong evidence** (of what the signer asserted) | Cryptographic; proves the signer declared AI generation, not that the scene is false. Signed "captured by camera" manifests are strong support for capture provenance | Yes: "This file carries a signed record from X saying it was made with AI" |
| C2PA absent | No value | Most platforms strip it | One collapsed line only |
| C2PA present but invalid or untrusted | Context-dependent | Could be an edited file or a forged manifest | Investigator |
| **SD / ComfyUI / InvokeAI parameter chunks** | **Strong clue, but forgeable** | Easy to *add* to a real photo. Currently treated as **proof** 🔴 | Yes, worded as "the file contains text claiming it was generated with Stable Diffusion (anyone can add this)" |
| **SD DWT-DCT reference watermark** | Useful corroboration; forgeable | The reference payload and encoder are public, so anyone can embed it into a real photo. Fragile under recompression; absence means nothing | Investigator. Never "proof" |
| IPTC DigitalSourceType = trainedAlgorithmicMedia | Useful corroboration (self-declared) | Set by some tools (Adobe, Google), editable | Yes, as "the file declares…" |
| EXIF Make/Model/Lens | Weak clue / context-dependent | Editable; stripped by platforms; mismatches are clues | Investigator |
| EXIF DateTimeOriginal | Weak clue | Editable, no timezone, camera clocks are wrong | Timeline only, as "file claims" |
| EXIF Software (Photoshop, Snapseed) | Useful context | Shows an editing pipeline, not manipulation of content | Investigator |
| JPEG quantisation tables | Context-dependent, useful for **provenance of the copy** | Identifies the encoder (WhatsApp, Instagram, a camera model, Photoshop). Very useful for genealogy and propagation ("this copy passed through WhatsApp"). Not an authenticity signal | Investigator; can feed "this copy was recompressed by …" |
| Double-JPEG / JPEG ghost | Context-dependent | Detects local recompression in camera-original JPEGs; destroyed by one platform recompression | Investigator |
| **ELA** | **Potentially misleading** | Responds to edges, texture and recompression; notorious for false "edited here" heatmaps | **Never** in the user view or verdict |
| Noise residual (generic) | Weak clue | Without a reference camera it is just texture statistics | Investigator |
| PRNU camera fingerprint | **Strong** (when a reference camera exists) | *Not implemented.* Valuable when the victim says "this is my photo, taken on my phone" | — |
| Spectral peaks | Weak clue / partly obsolete | GAN upsampling peaks; diffusion images and resizing produce or destroy peaks | Investigator |
| Face statistics | Weak clue | Count, size and landmarks are context | Investigator (face count is useful context) |
| Video temporal heuristics | Weak clue / potentially misleading | Compression, variable frame rate and dropped frames cause false flags | Investigator |
| Audio bandwidth / pause analysis | Weak clue / potentially misleading | Codec cut-offs (AMR, Opus) mimic TTS artefacts | Investigator |
| **OCR** | Useful context and leads | Handles, captions, platform watermarks ("Made with AI", TikTok ID, Instagram handle) | Yes, as "text visible in the image" |
| Whisper transcript | Useful context (not detection) | Multilingual content understanding; hallucinates on silence or music | Yes (transcript), with a caveat |
| **SPAI** | Useful corroboration | AUC 0.76 alone out-of-distribution [measured]; weak on new generators | Only inside "what each tool says" |
| **TruFor** | Useful corroboration on camera photos; heatmap valuable | False alarms on text overlays and heavy compression; not applicable to fully synthetic images | The heatmap, yes ("areas that don't match the rest of the photo"), only when an edit is the hypothesis |
| **SBI** | Context-dependent | Specialised for blending; noisy on real faces; blind to full synthesis, lip-sync and reenactment | Only as "face-swap check" when a face ≥ N px was found |
| UFD | Obsolete | GAN-era | Remove |
| **Hive** | Useful corroboration, the strongest single signal, but a **vendor opinion** | Closed, unreproducible, silent model updates; misses blended faces [measured] | Yes, as "commercial detector X says…" with its date and model version |

**Rule of thumb for the user view:** show only (a) provenance declarations, phrased as declarations;
(b) the detector *consensus statement*; (c) visual evidence a human can check (heatmap, side-by-side with the
original, crop box); (d) OCR text. Everything else belongs in the investigator view.

## 4. GENEALOGY audit

### What PDQ + pHash + SIFT/RANSAC + frame/audio alignment can establish

| Relationship | Reliability |
|---|---|
| **Same visual content** (near-duplicate) | **High** when PDQ distance is small **and** geometric verification finds many inliers over a non-template area |
| Crop / scale / mirror relation | **High** (the RANSAC homography is explicit, measurable evidence) |
| Caption / border added | High (the content region is matched, with extra pixels outside it) |
| Video segment / trim, audio reuse | High with frame-sequence plus audio-landmark alignment |
| **Direction (A → B)** | **Only suggestive**, except with asymmetric, irreversible evidence: B is a strict crop of A; B has lower resolution *and* A has detail B lacks; B carries A's caption plus more; B has extra recompression generations. Even then, **"B came from A" cannot be distinguished from "both came from an unseen C"** |
| "Original" / "source" | **Not establishable** from discovered copies |

### Where false relationships come from

- **Templates and memes:** same frame, different content.
- **The same stock photo** or **the same event** photographed by many people. This is especially a problem with
  CLIP-embedding similarity.
- **The same person in different photos.** Portraits with similar pose and background get low pHash distance.
- **Screenshots of the same app UI.** The chrome dominates the hash. The content crop helps, but only for images.
- **AI images from the same prompt or seed.**
- **Forced tree:** the Edmonds arborescence gives **every** member a parent [code]. That *manufactures* a direction
  and a lineage for siblings.
- **Proof contagion:** `family_provenance` with STRONG strength counts as verdict proof [code]. One wrong match plus
  one forgeable SD-parameter file can mark a real photo "Fake — proof".

### Evidence required before claiming two files are related

1. PDQ ≤ threshold **and** geometric verification (≥K inliers spread over ≥X% of the overlap), computed on the
   **content region**, not the UI chrome.
2. For direction: at least one *irreversible* asymmetry, stated explicitly. Otherwise the edge is **undirected**.
3. For video: aligned frame runs ≥ T seconds, not single frames.
4. A visual side-by-side with the matched keypoints, so a human can confirm.

### Terminology

| Current | Implies | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Family tree | Known parentage | **Copy map** / "related copies" |
| Root / origin / source | First creation | **Earliest copy found** · **most complete copy found** |
| Derived from | Proven causation | **Likely made from** (only with stated asymmetry) / **same content as** |
| Ancestor / child | Lineage | "larger version of" / "cropped version of" |

### How an investigator should read the graph

Clusters are reliable. Arrows are hypotheses. "Earliest" means earliest *we found*, and the system's
coverage is a small, biased slice of the internet.

## 5. PROPAGATION audit

### What capture actually gives

A **snapshot of what a logged-out headless Chromium in India saw at time T**:
- a screenshot, HTML and MHTML, headers and OG / JSON-LD metadata;
- visible handles and displayed counts;
- downloaded media (HTTP or yt-dlp).

### Does this reconstruct spread?

**No. It reconstructs presence.** It shows "this content was present at these locations at these declared and
observed times". Spread (who reshared from whom) needs platform repost graphs, which are unavailable. Specifically:

- **Displayed counts** are rounded and localised, may be personalised, and change. They are weak evidence.
- **Declared publish times** may be edit times or relative ("3h"), with timezone ambiguity.
- **Wayback** is sparse for social platforms and often fails behind login walls.
- **RDAP / DNS / ASN** are useful for **independent websites only**. For content on instagram.com the ASN is Meta,
  which tells you nothing. That output should be suppressed for platform URLs.
- **Login walls:** Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn often serve login pages. A capture of a login page must be
  labelled "content not visible", not "removed".
- **Geo / IP / VPN** affect what is served. The capture environment is **not recorded**.

### Status taxonomy for propagation

| Status | Meaning | Who can produce it |
|---|---|---|
| **Captured** | We hold bytes plus hash of what URL U served at time T (our clock) | DFIR |
| **Observed** | A human or the system saw it but has no full capture (e.g. a login wall) | DFIR / user |
| **Archived** | A third-party archive holds a copy dated T | Wayback, etc. |
| **Declared** | The page or file states something about itself (publish time, author) | Page metadata |
| **User-attested** | The victim says "I received this on WhatsApp from X on date D" | User (signed statement) |
| **Platform-reported** | The platform provided records (upload time, IP, account data) | Only via a lawful request |
| **Inferred** | Derived by reasoning (order of spread, direction) | DFIR |
| **Unknown** | An explicit gap | — |

The current five-level scheme folds these together. Most importantly, **user notes appear as "Verified"** [code].

### Where attribution goes wrong

1. **Found at ≠ created by.** Mostly respected in wording [code].
2. **Earliest found ≠ first posted.** Deleted posts, private groups, WhatsApp and Telegram are invisible.
3. **Look-alike handles.** The "look-alike" may be the victim's own backup account, a fan page, or a parody.
4. **Repost bots and aggregators** look like hubs.
5. **Impersonation of the victim:** the account *named* in the content is the victim, not the poster.
6. **Shared infrastructure:** a shared CDN, hosting or ASN means nothing.
7. **Platform-declared author** on aggregators is often wrong.

## 6. GRAPH audit

### What decision does it help with?

Only three legitimate questions:
1. **Which live locations should be reported first?** → a *list* ranked by status (live?), reach proxy and platform
   route, not a graph.
2. **Are these accounts likely one coordinated actor?** → needs ≥2 *independent* signals (same new upload within
   minutes, identical caption plus same media variant, linked bio URL, same contact number in OCR). A single
   similarity is never enough.
3. **Where else should I look?** → hashtags, linked domains, contact numbers seen in OCR.

### Dangers

- **Collection bias in centrality.** Betweenness is computed on *our* collection [code]. The node we searched from,
  or the hashtag we pasted, becomes "central". Centrality then reflects the investigator's process, not the actor's
  importance.
- **Hub nodes** (platform domains, popular hashtags, WhatsApp quantisation signature) connect everything into a
  spiderweb.
- **Look-alike handles** → false accusation risk.
- **Shared encoder signature** means "both went through WhatsApp/Instagram", which is shared by millions.
- **Generator signal** ("both Midjourney") links unrelated people.

### Edge rules

| Edge | Max status | Minimum evidence | Shown to |
|---|---|---|---|
| media–media same content | Verified (geometric) | PDQ + inliers on content region | Everyone (copy map) |
| media–media derived (directed) | Inferred | + stated irreversible asymmetry | Investigator |
| URL–media "carried" | Captured / Archived | Hash of downloaded bytes | Everyone |
| account–URL "posted" | Declared (page says) | Capture of the page showing the handle | Everyone, worded "appeared on account" |
| account–account "same operator" | Lead | ≥2 independent signals, each listed | Investigator only, behind a warning |
| account–account "look-alike handle" | Lead (weak) | String similarity **plus** context (impersonating the same victim) | Investigator only |
| shared encoder / generator / domain-hosting | Context (no edge by default) | — | Hidden; available as a filter |
| hashtag co-use | Context | — | Investigator only |

**Redesign:** replace the default graph view with three task views:
- **Takedown queue** (list);
- **Copy map** (clusters with thumbnails);
- **Account clusters** (only ≥2-signal clusters, each edge listing its evidence).

Keep the full graph as an "explore" tab for analysts.

## 7. TIMELINE audit

**Good** [code]:
- per-event time source and precision;
- "local time (timezone not recorded)";
- a hatched **unknown creation** band;
- "earliest discovered ≠ original";
- a metadata-vs-publication conflict check.

**Problems:**

1. **Points for imprecise times.** A day-precision or relative ("3h ago") publish time drawn as a dot at an exact
   instant reads as exact. Render **intervals sized to precision** (bars, whiskers), and hatch inferred times.
2. **Lanes mix time kinds.** Use separate, labelled lanes:
   - *Claimed by file* (EXIF / container);
   - *Declared by page* (published);
   - *First observed by us* (capture);
   - *Archived* (Wayback);
   - *Reported / acted* (complaints, takedowns);
   - *Platform-reported* (only via legal requests).
3. **Our own clock is assumed correct.** Record the NTP offset at capture and anchor critical events (capture,
   package) with RFC 3161.
4. **Statuses:** user notes and monitoring alerts are "Verified" [code]. They should be *User-attested* and
   *Captured*.
5. **Add the two bounds investigators actually use:**
   - *earliest proven existence*: the earliest Captured or Archived time (terminus ante quem);
   - *cannot have been created before* (terminus post quem): e.g. it depicts an event at date D, or the generator
     was released at date R (from Hive's source attribution, marked Inferred).
6. **Conflicts** should cover more than metadata vs publication: archive before declared publish, a capture showing
   different content than the archive, and so on.

## 8. EVIDENCE and CHAIN OF CUSTODY audit

### What holds up [code]

- SHA-256 + MD5 at acquisition; content-addressed, write-once vault.
- Per-file `acquisition.json` (method, URL, HTTP status, time pair).
- Hash-chained custody JSONL; `verify_package.py`; `hashes.csv`; manifest with all claims, timeline, graph and gaps.
- Case file PDF/HTML; draft BSA s.63 certificate listing file hashes.
- The safety gate stops minors' media from being downloaded.

### Integrity vs authenticity

The package can show **the collected bytes are unchanged since collection**. It says **nothing** about whether the
depicted content is real. The case file and certificate must state this in one sentence at the top. Today it is
implied by the README, not stated in the package itself.

### Missing or weak

| Gap | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **No external anchoring** (RFC 3161 consent exists, not implemented) | A hash chain proves internal consistency only. Whoever controls the laptop can regenerate the whole chain | RFC 3161 timestamp of the ledger head at each export, plus the package hash (FreeTSA or another TSA); store the `.tsr` in the package |
| **Analyst identity** is a default string | Custody needs *who* | Local user profile (name, role, organisation), recorded per action; separate "automated" vs "human" actors |
| **Clock** | Times rest on the system clock | Record the NTP offset and timezone per session; ISO-8601 with offset everywhere |
| **Assessment not frozen** | The verdict is recomputed live from current calibration and engine | Store an immutable **assessment record**: inputs (claim IDs plus hashes), engine (hive/local), model and weight hashes, calibration file hash, config flags, output. Add it to the vault and ledger |
| **Hive raw response and task_id discarded** | Vendor opinion can't be traced | Store the full JSON response, `task_id`, model and version, endpoint and request time in the vault |
| **Tool and model provenance** absent from the package | Reproducibility | Include `models.lock.json`, `calibration.json`, `hive_eval.json`, `uv.lock`, `third_party/PATCHES.md` + commits, OS/GPU/driver |
| **Capture environment** | What was served depends on IP, geo, UA and login state | Record public IP / ASN, VPN flag, UA, viewport, logged-out state, Playwright version |
| **MHTML only** | Not the archival standard | Add **WARC** (ISO 28500) via a WARC-writing proxy; keep MHTML for convenience |
| **How the victim obtained the file** | Chain of custody starts *before* upload | A mandatory "How did you receive this?" form, stored as User-attested, plus "Do you have the original message/chat?" |
| **Encryption at rest** | NCII/CSAM-adjacent material sits unencrypted on a laptop (no encryption code found) [code] | Encrypt the vault per case (key from passphrase); access log; secure delete on case closure; DPDP Act consent and retention note |
| **BSA s.63 certificate** | The draft lists hashes (good), but must follow the statutory Schedule (Part A by the party, Part B by an expert) and name the person in control of the device | Generate both parts with signature blocks and explicit instructions on who signs |
| **Derived copies** | Crops, PNG crops sent to Hive, thumbnails | Store every derived artefact with its parent hash and transform, e.g. "PNG crop of original box (x0,y0,x1,y1) sent to Hive" |
| **Verify script for humans** | Police won't run Python | Also produce a **one-page "Verification sheet" PDF**: package SHA-256, TSA timestamp, how to check with built-in OS tools (`certutil -hashfile` / `shasum`) |

## 9. CLAIMS audit

The five statuses are a strong base, but they mix three axes:
1. **source type**: observed, declared, attested, inferred;
2. **corroboration**: single vs multiple independent sources;
3. **strength**.

They also lack *contradiction* and *human review*.

### Proposed model

```
Claim
  statement            "Image B is a cropped copy of image A"
  kind                 Observation | Declaration | Attestation | Inference | Assessment
  status               Captured · Verified(crypto/geometry) · Corroborated · Inferred · Lead ·
                       Disputed · Unknown · Superseded
  supporting[]         evidence items (hash refs, artefacts, measurements)
  contradicting[]      evidence items against it (required field; empty must be stated explicitly)
  method               "PDQ + SIFT/RANSAC homography on content regions"
  tool@version         genealogy.match@1.3, opencv 4.x, config hash
  inputs               sha256 of A, B
  produced_by          automated | analyst:<name>
  at                   ISO time + clock source
  strength             strong / moderate / weak (for this claim type, defined per method)
  limitations          "Cannot distinguish 'B from A' vs 'A and B from unseen C'"
  alternatives[]       competing explanations considered
  would_confirm        "Original upload file from the platform; EXIF of A matching camera"
  would_refute         "A version of B with content outside A's frame"
  next_step            "Request upload records for A from Instagram via police"
  reviewed_by          analyst sign-off (optional), with time
```

## 10. VERDICT audit

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is "Real" ever justified? | **Almost never from detectors.** Absence of detected manipulation is not authenticity. A signed C2PA capture manifest from a trusted camera, a matching PRNU camera fingerprint, or independent eyewitness corroboration can *support* authenticity; detectors cannot. "Real" also misleads on real photos with false captions |
| Is "99% likely fake" understandable? | People read it as "99% sure", i.e. certain. They don't know 99% of *what*, against which reference population, or with what prior |
| What does the number represent? | Today: a mix. A Hive class score (vendor-internal) or a balanced-prior logistic output, combined by an independence formula, clipped to 1–99. **It is not a calibrated probability for the user's case** |
| Should the UI show a percentage? | **Not as the headline.** Show it in the investigator view, labelled precisely, e.g. "Hive score 0.99 (vendor scale); on our test set, 36/37 fakes and 0/50 real photos scored this high" |

### Recommendation (scientifically safest, still simple)

Use **two separate questions**, each with a **verbal evidence-strength scale** (in the style of ENFSI evaluative
reporting) and an explicit **"what this does not tell you"**.

**1. Was the picture made or altered by AI or editing?**
- **Proof of AI generation**: valid signed C2PA from a trusted signer only.
- **Strong evidence of manipulation**: ≥2 independent detectors agree, or 1 strong detector plus visible evidence
  (heatmap, original-photo comparison).
- **Some evidence of manipulation**: one detector, or detectors disagree with a specialised one firing.
- **No signs of manipulation found**: "this does **not** confirm it's genuine".
- **Can't assess**: audio, unsupported format, too small, screenshot of a video, and so on.

**2. Is the claim around it true?** (place, date, people, event)
- Separate panel: reverse-search results, earliest copy found, context checks. Usually *"not checked"* until the
  user runs search.

**Confidence becomes "How much should you rely on this?"**, from applicability (face found? resolution OK?
screenshot?), agreement and measured reliability on a **named** reference set.

**Example headline:** *"Strong evidence this image was made with AI. Two independent detectors agree. This doesn't
tell us who made it or where it was first posted."*

## 12. The "next step" problem: the biggest opportunity

**Key insight:** for most victims, **"is it fake?" is not the deciding question.**
- NCII is removable under Rule 3(2)(b) whether real or morphed.
- Impersonation is removable without proving synthesis.
- A fraud freeze depends on time, not on detection.

Detection matters most for journalists, disputes and evidentiary assessment. DFIR has incident types and static
playbooks [code], but they sit **behind** the detector and in the 10th sidebar item.

### Proposal: triage-first

1. **Five questions, 30 seconds:**
   - Is anyone in immediate danger?
   - Is it sexual or intimate?
   - Could a minor be involved?
   - Has money moved, or been asked for?
   - Is it pretending to be you, or someone you work for?
2. **"Your next 10 minutes"** checklist, by incident:

| Incident | Next 10 minutes |
|---|---|
| **Intimate deepfake** | You're not at fault · don't forward it · capture the URLs (we'll do it) · report as intimate/morphed imagery from the person depicted (2-hour rule) · StopNCII from your own phone · NCRP Women/Child route · support helpline (Tele-MANAS 14416) · *we won't upload your image anywhere* |
| **Minor involved** | Don't download or forward · report URL only · NCRP anonymous route · 1098 / 112 · Take It Down |
| **Money / fraud** | Call 1930 now · call your bank's fraud line · keep chats and transaction IDs · don't delete · warn contacts through your own channel |
| **CEO / org impersonation** | Freeze payments pending call-back verification · alert finance and assistants · preserve the message trail · report the account as impersonation · holding statement · check CERT-In reportability · legal counsel |
| **Political / misinformation** | Don't amplify · capture · report as manipulated media · journalists: publication guidance · avoid naming accounts |
| **Harassment / impersonation** | Report the profile · capture the profile page (creation date, bio) · NCRP Other cyber crime · tell your contacts |

3. **Then** the evidence pack, and **then** detection, which is shown as supporting evidence.

**This is a meaningful product opportunity and the most demonstrable change.**

## 13. MONITORING audit

**Today** [code]: periodic re-capture of known URLs (removed / restored / changed) plus new archive snapshots. That
is useful for **takedown compliance**, weak for **recurrence**.

**Can it detect new re-uploads elsewhere?** Only through search adapters with keys, and only on the open web. It
cannot see private groups, WhatsApp, logged-in Instagram or Telegram private channels.

### Useful alert model

| Alert | Trigger |
|---|---|
| **Takedown overdue** | Reported at T; still live after the legal or SLA window (2 h / 36 h / 7 d) → "escalate to Grievance Officer → GAC" with a pre-filled text |
| **Removed** | Good news; record the evidence of removal (a capture showing 404 or "unavailable", distinguished from a login wall) |
| **Restored / re-uploaded at same URL** | Restored or re-uploaded at the same URL |
| **New copy found** | PDQ/geometric match from a reverse search (daily or weekly); include crops, mirrors and screenshots via content-region hashing |
| **Material change** | Caption now names the victim, adds contact details or a threat; the media variant changed |

**Do NOT alert on:** count jitter, ad or layout HTML changes, relative-time text changes, CDN URL rotation,
login-wall flicker.

**Honest coverage statement in the UI:** "We check N URLs every X hours and search the public web daily via
<sources>. We cannot see private chats, private groups or content behind logins."

## 14. Missing capabilities that would materially help

1. **Original-photo comparison ("find the real photo").** Most personal deepfakes reuse the victim's own public photo.
   If the user uploads or links the original, alignment plus a difference map produces *"Background, body and clothing
   identical; face region replaced"*. That is the **most convincing evidence** for police, platforms and journalists,
   and it is checkable by a layperson. Genealogy already has the alignment machinery.
2. **Context verification for viral media:** guided reverse search (generate Google Lens / Bing / Yandex / TinEye
   links for the user to run, plus API when keyed), first-appearance logging, a geolocation and chronolocation
   checklist, and publication-language templates.
3. **Audio deepfake assessment:** Hive's audio classes, Reality Defender audio (free tier), plus *process
   verification* guidance (call-back), which beats any detector for CEO fraud.
4. **Talking-head video:** AV-sync / lip-sync consistency check; frame-sampled image detectors on faces.
5. **Takedown tracker with legal clocks** (§13).
6. **Law-enforcement request kit:** which platform holds which records (subscriber info, IP logs, upload original,
   creation date), drafted for the officer's lawful notice. No API, just a well-structured document.
7. **Capture standardisation:** WARC, capture environment, profile-page capture, an RFC 3161 anchor (in the spirit of
   the Berkeley Protocol).
8. **Case encryption, access control and a retention policy** (DPDP Act 2023 for personal data).
9. **Human review and sign-off** on key claims before export (a "peer reviewed by" field).
10. **Product evaluation harness** (§18).

## 21. The four personas

### What an experienced DFIR examiner would not trust

- Any percentage, and the word "Real".
- "Proof" from PNG text chunks or a public watermark.
- A verdict that isn't frozen with its inputs.
- Hive scores without a stored raw response, model version or `task_id`.
- Genealogy arrows without stated asymmetry.
- Centrality from my own collection.
- "Verified" on user statements.
- A custody chain anchored only to the same laptop.
- Capture without its network environment.
- ELA in any report.

### Where it fails a victim

- It makes me wait for, and read, a forensic verdict before telling me what to do.
- It may tell me my fake is "Real".
- It may upload my intimate image to a company without asking.
- It shows yesterday's deadlines.
- It gives me a ZIP I can't use and a certificate I don't know who signs.
- It can't see the copies spreading in WhatsApp groups (honestly, nothing can). It should say so, and tell me
  StopNCII and the platforms' 2-hour route are my best tools.

### What a cybercrime officer still does manually

- FIR registration and the legal sections.
- Notices to platforms for subscriber, IP and upload records, and preservation requests through law-enforcement
  portals.
- Correlating with bank and telecom records.
- Section 63 certificate signatures (Part A / Part B).
- Expert examination if contested.
- Identifying people.
- Everything behind logins.

DFIR could pre-draft the *what-to-request-from-whom* list. Today it doesn't.

### Hackathon judge

**Genuinely impressive:**
- The claim / status / limitations discipline.
- Screenshot-aware detection with measured before/after (0.00 → 0.9997).
- Leak-guarded held-out evaluation.
- The custody vault plus verify script.
- Minors safety gate.
- Copy matching across crop, mirror and screen recording.
- An NCRP-aware response flow.

**Complexity disguised as innovation:**
- The relationship graph and centrality.
- ELA, spectral and noise panels.
- Five detectors fused by an ad-hoc formula into one percentage.
- A forced "family tree".
- RDAP/ASN on platform URLs.
- The 12-step sidebar.

**Demo the response, not the spiderweb.**

## 22. The 10 highest-impact changes

Ranked by impact × reliability × real-world usefulness × novelty × demonstrability.

### 1. Triage-first "Your next 10 minutes" home screen

- **Current behaviour:** upload → detection → verdict; playbooks are the 10th sidebar item.
- **Problem:** the victim's urgent actions don't depend on detection, so the product delays them.
- **New behaviour:**
  - 5 triage questions → incident type + safety flags → a time-ordered checklist (preserve, report route with the
    legal clock, StopNCII / 1930 / bank, don't forward, support);
  - detection runs in the background and appears as one card;
  - every checklist item has a "done" state that is logged in custody.
- **Why it matters:** it directly improves *time-to-next-correct-action*, the metric that matters most.
- **Technical implementation:**
  - extend `reasoning/next_action.py` `INCIDENT_GUIDANCE` into a versioned playbook YAML (steps, owner, deadline,
    route, evidence needed);
  - add a triage form on case creation;
  - checklist state lives in the DB;
  - steps add `TimelineNote` entries (User-attested).
- **UI:** the first screen after case creation. A big checklist with one primary button per step ("Open NCRP — Women/Child route", "Copy report text"); a progress ring; the detection card on the right.
- **Evidence required:** expert-reviewed playbooks (a cyber-cell officer or NGO), each step citing its source and an "as of" date.
- **Risk / limitation:** legal routes change. Mitigate with dated sources and a "verify" link per step; this is not
  legal advice.

### 2. Close the outward-transmission hole (safety gate on every upload)

- **Current behaviour:** Hive auto-upload by default; the safety flags only block downloading discovered media.
- **Problem:** intimate imagery or CSAM is sent to a third party without informed consent; legal exposure.
- **New behaviour:**
  - `intimate` or `minors_possible` → **all outward analysis is hard-blocked** (minors) or requires explicit
    per-file consent with a plain-language explanation (intimate);
  - the default becomes per-case consent;
  - a "local-only mode" toggle on case creation.
- **Why it matters:** safety and trust are prerequisites; one incident would end the product.
- **Technical implementation:** a `SafetyPolicy.may_upload_external` check in `HiveDetector._consented` and in all
  `_ApiDetector` classes; consent text from `OUTWARD_ACTIONS`; the ledger records the consent ID.
- **UI:** a "This will send the image to Hive (US company). Not sent yet." card with Send / Keep local; a lock icon
  on local-only cases.
- **Evidence required:** consent records in custody.
- **Risk / limitation:** less Hive coverage, so the local verdict is used more. That is acceptable and honest.

### 3. Replace the "Fake/Real + %" headline with a two-question evidence-strength assessment

- **Current behaviour:** a 5-label verdict, % likely fake/real, a "right 99%" reliability line.
- **Problem:** not a probability; says "Real"; ignores context; over-generalises.
- **New behaviour:**
  - Q1, "Made or altered by AI or editing?": Proof / Strong evidence / Some evidence / No signs found
    (authenticity not established) / Can't assess, with "detectors disagree" as a first-class state.
  - Q2, "Is the claim around it true?": a context panel, "not checked" by default.
  - Reliability is shown in natural frequencies on a named test set.
- **Why it matters:** removes the most misleading output; matches forensic reporting practice (verbal scales); still
  simple for users.
- **Technical implementation:**
  - rework `analyzers/verdict.py` outputs: `assessment`, `strength`, `agreement`, `applicability`;
  - per-detector likelihood ratios from `calibration.json` / `hive_eval.json`;
  - keep `p_fake` for the investigator view only.
- **UI:** a headline sentence + an evidence stack (supporting / against / not applicable chips) + "what this does
  not prove" + "next step". The percentage moves into "Details".
- **Evidence required:** the held-out evaluation per detector, with CIs; a disagreement study.
- **Risk / limitation:** users wanting a yes/no may find it softer. User-test the wording; the "Strong evidence"
  phrasing is still decisive.

### 4. "Compare with the original photo"

- **Current behaviour:** genealogy only links copies the user collected; no guided original comparison.
- **Problem:** the most convincing proof of a personal deepfake (the untouched source photo) is never asked for.
- **New behaviour:** "Do you know the original photo this was made from? Upload it or paste its link." Align
  (SIFT / RANSAC), compute a difference map, report: "92% of the image matches your original; the face region
  differs".
- **Why it matters:** a layperson, a police officer and a platform reviewer can all *see* it; it doesn't depend on
  detector generalisation.
- **Technical implementation:** reuse `genealogy/match.py` homography; difference on the aligned, colour-normalised
  images; mask morphology; region summary via face boxes.
- **UI:** a before/after slider with the changed region outlined; one sentence.
- **Evidence required:** both files vaulted; the transform and difference artefact stored with parameters.
- **Risk / limitation:** only works when the source exists and is found; heavy re-generation (img2img) changes
  everything. Say so.

### 5. Frozen, reproducible assessment records, with an external anchor

- **Current behaviour:** the verdict is computed at view time; Hive raw output is not kept; the package has no model
  or config hashes; the ledger is not anchored.
- **Problem:** not reproducible or defensible; results can silently change.
- **New behaviour:**
  - each analysis writes an immutable assessment JSON to the vault: inputs, engine, model hashes, calibration hash,
    config, full Hive response with `task_id`, output;
  - the package includes the locks, calibration, `uv.lock` and patches;
  - an RFC 3161 timestamp of the ledger head plus the package hash at export;
  - analyst identity and NTP offset are recorded.
- **Why it matters:** this turns analysis into evidence.
- **Technical implementation:**
  - `pipeline.analyze_media` → `vault.store(kind="assessment")`;
  - `reporting/package.py` adds files;
  - implement the existing `rfc3161` consent with the `rfc3161ng` library or an `openssl ts` request to a TSA.
- **UI:** "Assessment #3 · frozen 08 Oct 14:02 IST · timestamped by FreeTSA"; a diff view when re-run.
- **Evidence required:** the TSA response (`.tsr`) and the TSA certificate in the package.
- **Risk / limitation:** TSA availability; it timestamps hashes, not truth.

### 6. Takedown tracker with legal clocks, and honest copy monitoring

- **Current behaviour:** re-capture of known URLs; static legal text (stale 24 h).
- **Problem:** the victim doesn't know whether the platform is late or what to do next; new copies go unseen.
- **New behaviour:**
  - per location: reported-at (user-attested), route, applicable clock (2 h intimate/morphed, grievance 7 d,
    GAC 30 d), live status from monitoring, "Overdue → escalate" with a pre-filled Grievance Officer / GAC text;
  - daily copy discovery via reverse search (assisted manual links + API when keyed) with PDQ / content-region
    matching;
  - an explicit coverage statement.
- **Why it matters:** this is what actually gets content down. It is demonstrable live (the demo already has a
  "taken down" page).
- **Technical implementation:**
  - `monitor/scheduler.py` + a new `Takedown` model;
  - legal clocks in a versioned YAML with sources and `as_of`;
  - login-wall detection in capture.
- **UI:** a board with columns Live / Reported / Overdue / Removed / Restored; red clocks.
- **Evidence required:** captures before and after; the user's acknowledgement numbers.
- **Risk / limitation:** platform-specific timings and rule interpretation. Mark as "verify with a lawyer".

### 7. Demote forgeable signals, and stop proof contagion

- **Current behaviour:** SD parameters and the reference watermark are "Verified STRONG" and override everything;
  family provenance inherits proof.
- **Problem:** the liar's-dividend attack. A real photo can be made "provably fake".
- **New behaviour:**
  - only a valid C2PA signature from a trusted signer counts as *proof* (of the assertion);
  - SD parameters and watermarks become "Declared — forgeable", and are cross-checked against pixel detectors;
  - a conflict ("file claims AI, pixels look like a camera") is itself a finding;
  - no inheritance across genealogy.
- **Why it matters:** removes the system's most dangerous false-certainty path.
- **Technical implementation:** `verdict.features_from_claims` proof rules; `provenance_signals` status changes; a
  test for the attack.
- **UI:** "The file *says* it was made with Stable Diffusion. Anyone can add this text."
- **Evidence required:** test cases with injected metadata.
- **Risk / limitation:** genuine SD outputs lose "proof" status. That is correct; detectors still support them.

### 8. Honest copy map (genealogy) and claim-model upgrade

- **Current behaviour:** forced arborescence; "root/origin"; five statuses mixing source type and strength; notes
  shown as "Verified".
- **Problem:** manufactured lineage; misreadable certainty.
- **New behaviour:**
  - undirected same-content clusters; arrows only with stated irreversible asymmetry;
  - vocabulary: "earliest copy found" / "largest copy found";
  - claims gain `kind`, `contradicting[]`, `alternatives`, `would_refute`, `reviewed_by`;
  - new statuses: User-attested, Captured, Platform-reported, Disputed.
- **Why it matters:** defensibility and fewer false inferences.
- **Technical implementation:** `genealogy/tree.py` (drop forced parentage; keep a maximum spanning forest only for
  layout); `core/claims.py` schema with migration; timeline status mapping.
- **UI:** the claim card in §9; copy map clusters with "same content" lines and occasional evidence-labelled arrows.
- **Evidence required:** a genealogy benchmark with hard negatives.
- **Risk / limitation:** a less "story-like" picture. That is the point.

### 9. Audio and video coverage, plus process verification

- **Current behaviour:** no audio detection; video is Hive-only; voice-clone incident type exists.
- **Problem:** the most common fraud deepfake (voice) is unassessable, silently.
- **New behaviour:**
  - send audio to Hive (classes already present in its response) and/or Reality Defender (free audio tier);
  - an AV-sync check for talking-head video;
  - frame-sampled face checks;
  - "can't assess" stated plainly;
  - a playbook step: "verify by calling back on a known number".
- **Why it matters:** Case D and voice scams.
- **Technical implementation:** `HiveDetector.modalities` += audio with the same endpoint; a SyncNet-style
  lip-sync scorer (research licence check); frame sampler → SBI/SPAI.
- **UI:** "Voice check: …" card; "Lip-sync: not checked / consistent / inconsistent".
- **Evidence required:** an evaluation on recent TTS clones (current benchmarks lag behind 2024–25 TTS) [research].
- **Risk / limitation:** audio detectors generalise poorly. Lead with process verification.

### 10. Product-level evaluation harness

- **Current behaviour:** model metrics on OpenFake; 8 demo images.
- **Problem:** nothing measures whether people make the right decision, or the false-accusation rate.
- **New behaviour:** 12 scenario cards (A–D incident types and the §17 attacks). Measure decision correctness,
  time-to-next-action, comprehension, false-accusation rate, unconsented-transmission count (= 0), and
  reproducibility. Publish the numbers in-app.
- **Why it matters:** it proves usefulness, not just model AUC; strong for judges.
- **Technical implementation:**
  - `scripts/scenarios/` with fixtures (synthetic and consented);
  - a pytest suite for the attacks (metadata forgery, contagion, screenshot, low-res);
  - a small user study protocol (8–10 people, 20 min).
- **UI:** a Lab "Product benchmark" tab.
- **Evidence required:** expert answer keys (an officer or lawyer); participant consent.
- **Risk / limitation:** small samples. Report CIs and don't over-claim.
